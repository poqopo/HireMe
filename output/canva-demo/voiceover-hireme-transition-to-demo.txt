So that's the idea.

HireMe is not asking creators to give away the private Harness that makes their Agent valuable.
And it is not asking clients to leave their actual workflow just to use a specialist Agent.

The client hires the work.
The creator keeps the know-how.
And HireMe sits in the middle as the protected execution boundary.

Now let's make that concrete.

In this demo, we will start as a client on the HireMe web app.
The client chooses a specialized Agent, sends a real request, and receives the final result.

Then we will use the same hired Agent through MCP, directly from the workspace where the client already works.

As the demo continues, watch for three proof points:
the client gets the output, not the private Harness;
Walrus stores the protected Agent artifact as durable proof;
and MemWal lets the hired Agent remember context across sessions and multi-Agent workflows.
